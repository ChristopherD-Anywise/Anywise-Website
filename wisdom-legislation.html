01

Legislation is written for people, not systems

An Act is a single long document. Software can search its text, but it cannot reliably answer the questions that actually matter.

Ask a system what paragraph (c) of subsection (2) of section 387 requires, or what else is affected if a provision changes, and it has no dependable way to answer. Text search returns passages, not provisions. Nothing knows where a clause sits, what it depends on, or what depends on it.

As published

One continuous text. Provisions have no addresses. Cross-references are prose. Scope and hierarchy are visual conventions only a person can interpret.

Structured

Every provision individually addressable, aware of exactly where it sits, and linked to the provisions it depends on. Queryable, citable, traceable.

02

What we produce

We convert an Act into structured data in which every provision, down to the smallest sub-clause, becomes a precisely addressable unit. The result behaves as three things at once, from a single delivered artifact.

A document

A hierarchy

A network

04

Precision to the clause

Consider a real question: what must the Fair Work Commission consider when deciding whether a dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable? Each criterion is a separately addressable provision.

s.387Criteria for considering harshness etc.
s.387(0)In considering whether it is satisfied that a dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable, the FWC must take into account:
(a)whether there was a valid reason for the dismissal related to the person's capacity or conduct
(b)whether the person was notified of that reason
(c)whether the person was given an opportunity to respond
(e)if the dismissal related to unsatisfactory performance, whether the person had been warned
(h)any other matters that the FWC considers relevant

A system can point to the exact clause that establishes a warning requirement, rather than returning section 387 and leaving a person to find it. That is the difference between a search result and an answer you can rely on.

Proven, and repeatable. Produced to date for the Fair Work Act 2009 and the Competition and Consumer Act 2010, using the same process with no Act-specific rework. Extending to further legislation is a processing exercise, not a development one.
