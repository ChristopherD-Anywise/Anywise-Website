Capabilities

The problems we’re built to solve.

Five areas where operational complexity outpaces the tools available to deal with it, and where we've built platforms, services and teams to close that gap.

We don't lead with a product catalogue, because the problem comes first. Each capability below describes a class of problem we understand deeply, what we do about it, and the platforms and services we bring to bear. Start with the problem that sounds like yours.

Our capability areas

Decision Augmentation

The information is there. The decision still isn't.

Commanders and executives are drowning in data and starved of judgement-ready insight. We build systems that fuse fragmented sources, reason over them, and hand back a recommendation with the evidence attached, so a person can trust it or overrule it.

Explore decision augmentation →

Sensing

You can't act on what you can't see.

The places that matter most are often the ones you can't watch: no power, no network, no easy access. We design and field low-cost, low-power sensors you can deploy in numbers and leave in place, and the systems that turn what they detect into a live picture.

Explore sensing →

Asset Management

Buying the capability is the easy part. Keeping it is not.

Complex platforms spend decades in service and most of their cost after delivery. We make systems supportable through life, with the engineering, logistics and monitoring that keep availability up and surprises down.

Explore asset management →

Data

Fragmented records, unproven outcomes, undetected risk.

Programme data usually arrives inconsistent, incomplete and too late to steer anything. We build the collection standards, detection engines and reporting pipelines that turn it into evidence: what's working, what isn't, and where integrity is at risk.

Explore data →

Innovation

Some problems don't have a product yet.

We invest in the problems others park as too hard, and run our own R&D to work them out: denied networks, no power, data no one can act on in time. We prove the difficult part first, test it against the real environment, then keep developing until it holds up in operators' hands.

Explore innovation →
